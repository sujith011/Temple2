The temple

The fierce mother who faces north

In the sanctum stands Bhadrakali in her ugra form — about six to seven feet, carved from jackfruit wood, eight-armed, holding the head of the demon Darika, a sickle-sword, an anklet, a bell, and other weapons. Devotees know her as Sree Kurumba, the mother of this town.

The compound is Kerala architecture on about ten acres: a Shiva shrine without the usual Nandi, the seven mothers in a dark western chamber, a pond said to have been struck open by her sword, and a smallpox shrine still rubbed with turmeric.

Tradition holds that Adi Shankara seated the temple’s power in chakras behind the image, and that the priest worships those yantras facing east — not the wooden murti herself.

Sthalapurana

Two stories the temple keeps

The shrine holds two founding legends. The website does not choose between them. Both are living tradition.

Parasurama and Darika

After Parasurama raised Kerala from the sea, the demon Darika tormented the land. From Shiva’s eyes Bhadrakali was born. She slew the demon, and Parasurama installed her here as Parashakti — the fiercest of four sisters he is said to have seated in the south.

Kannagi and Senguttuvan

The Tamil epic Silappatikaram brings Kannagi north after Madurai burns. Chera king Senguttuvan is believed to have built her a shrine at Kodungallur about 1,800 years ago. Devotees still offer an anklet to Amma.

Muziris, then Kodungallur

The town around the temple is ancient Muziris — a Chera port known to Greeks and Romans, later Mahodayapuram. The temple stands in that old capital, about 1.5 km from today’s bus stand.

Utsavam

Festivals that still shake the town

Bharani

From the Bharani of Kumbham to seven days after the Bharani of Meenam. Oracles in red, Kavu Theendal, turmeric on the roof, and a week of purification. Animal sacrifice is banned; the old rite is now symbolic.

Festival calendar →

Thalappoli

Four days from Makara Sankranti. Elephants, barrel crackers, and women carrying lamps of rice and coconut. Malayarayan, Kudumbi, and the Kovilakam each keep a day.

Navaratri & Chandattam

The wooden image is protected each year by Chandattam, a bath of sacred adhesive. Navaratri brings the mother as Saraswati, Lakshmi, and Durga in turn.

Darshan

Open before dawn

The nada opens at 4:00 AM with Malar Nivedyam. Ucha pooja is at 11:30. The temple rests from noon to 4:00 PM, then Deeparadhana at 6:15 and Athazha pooja at 7:30. Guruthy follows the night closing.

Full timetable & vazhipadu

Tuesdays, Fridays, and the Sabarimala season are the busiest days. Traditional dress is expected. Book Nakshatra Pushpanjali or Guruthy a few days ahead.
