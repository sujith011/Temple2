## 2. The two origin stories (and how they sit together)

The temple holds **two overlapping founding legends**. The website should present both as tradition, not choose one as “the” history.

### 2.1 Parasurama and the slaying of Darika

After Parasurama created Kerala, the demon **Darikan / Darika** tormented him. Parasurama prayed to Shiva. Bhadrakali emerged from Shiva’s three eyes and slew the demon. Parasurama is said to have installed the goddess here; devotees take the deity as **Parashakti** herself. (Wikipedia, citing Janam TV.)

A related Kerala Tourism festival note says Parasurama established four Bhagavathy temples:

1. Kanyakumari — _Balambika_
2. Kodungallur — _Lokambika_ (the fiercest of the four)
3. Palakkad — _Hemambika_
4. Kollur (Karnataka) — _Mookambika_

### 2.2 Kannagi and Cheran Senguttuvan (Silappatikaram)

A second stream ties the shrine to **Kannagi / Kannaki**, heroine of Ilango Adigal’s Tamil epic _Silappatikaram_:

- Kannagi’s husband **Kovalan** was wrongly executed in Madurai for the theft of a queen’s anklet.
- In grief she tore off her left breast and burned Madurai.
- She is believed to have come north to the Chera country. Chera king **Senguttuvan (Cheran Chenguttuvan)**, ruling from Kodungallur, is said to have moved her a furlong south of the present site and built a shrine.
- Consecration is described as **Shakta**: a thousand pots of toddy and a thousand chickens. Because of the goddess’s power she was later installed at the present site with **Shiva facing south, Parashakti facing north, Bhadrakali facing west**.
- Tradition dates Kannagi’s installation to about **1,800 years ago**.
- Kannagi is believed to seek _moksha_ and union with Bhadrakali in the sanctum. Before Kodungallur she is said to have given darshan at **Attukal**, where women pacified her with _pongala_ — the origin of Attukal Pongala. (Wikipedia; Kerala Tourism Muziris; Vanamali Ashram.)

Kerala Tourism’s Muziris page calls Kodungallur the **second abode of Kannaki**, and says the idol of Kannaki was discovered about 1,800 years ago by Cheran Chenguttuvan.

**Caution for the website:** the Kerala Tourism festival PDF itself says _historians disagree_ with a simple “this is Kannagi’s temple” identification. Keep the Kannagi story as **sacred narrative**, and keep the archaeology / Chera-port history in a separate voice.

### 2.3 Adi Shankara and the chakras

Adi Shankaracharya is believed to have consecrated **chakras** (accounts say three _Sri Chakras_ or five chakras) that hold the temple’s power. Kerala Tourism Muziris: “the magnificent power of the temple is conceived in the five Chakras consecrated by Adi Shankaracharya.” Vanamali Ashram: the _pujari_ sits facing **east**, worshipping three Sri Chakras installed by Shankara, not facing the wooden image; a door opposite the chakras opens only on special days or when the Raja of Kodungallur comes at dawn.

### 2.4 Later history (festival PDF)

- During **Mysorean / Tipu** invasions the temple was destroyed; the power of the goddess was invoked into a sword and kept in a nearby _illam_; after Tipu retreated the temple was renovated.
- Belonged to **Kodungallur Kovilakam until 1949**; Cochin Devaswom Board from **1950**.
- Vanamali Ashram records that Kodungallur was among the first Kerala temples to allow entry of all castes, **before** the Temple Entry Proclamation.

Chicken / rooster sacrifice has been **banned since 1978** under the Kerala Animals and Birds Sacrifices Prohibition Act (Wikipedia, citing _The Hindu_). Symbolic _Kozhikkallu moodal_ continues as covering stones with red silk.

## 3. The town around the temple

Kodungallur is ancient **Muziris** (Greek/Roman), _Muciri / Muchiripattanam_ in Tamil, later _Mahodayapuram / Thiruvanchikulam_; the British called it **Cranganore**. The Kerala Tourism festival PDF lists older names (Jangli, Gingaleh, Cyngilin, Shinkali, etc.) and a folk etymology _Kodi-linga-puram_ (“land of 10 million Shiva lingas”). Historians also connect the name to a Chera customs post (_Kudakonallur_).

The Periyar flood of **1341 CE** wrecked the port. The town is the traditional landing of **St. Thomas** (1st century) and the site of **Cheraman Juma Masjid**, often called India’s first mosque. This context belongs on a “Visit Kodungallur” page, not as temple doctrine.

Compiled mainly from e-Kshetra, Vanamali Ashram, AstroVed, and Wikipedia.

| Feature                | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Main sanctum           | Bhadrakali, north-facing, jackfruit-wood, eight-armed                                                                                                                                    |
| Shiva shrine           | East of / beside the sanctum; **no Nandi** in the usual place. Vanamali: puja is done **first to Shiva, then to the goddess**; instructions said to come from the goddess                |
| Sapthamatrikas         | Western chamber of the inner temple: Brahmi, Maheswari, Vaishnavi, Varahi, Narasimhi, Kaumari, Aindri, Chamunda — facing north                                                           |
| Ganapati               | Inside the main temple, facing east                                                                                                                                                      |
| Veerabhadra            | Adjacent NE chamber, facing west                                                                                                                                                         |
| Kannagi shrine         | Separate shrine for Kannagi as an incarnation of Durga (AstroVed)                                                                                                                        |
| Vysoori / Vasuri mala  | West / outer courtyard. Medieval smallpox / contagious-disease shrine. Turmeric is rubbed on the walls. _Vasuri_ = smallpox; the image wears a garland of pustules. Rice bran is offered |
| Kshetrapala            | Unusually large gate-guardian on the eastern approach                                                                                                                                    |
| Secret granite chamber | East of the sanctum, behind the yantras; never opened. Folk memory: treasure of ancient kings; a secret passage toward Thiruvanchikulam Shiva temple (which has a Kovalan shrine)        |
| Pushkarini             | Sacred pond ~50 m away. Tradition: the goddess struck the ground with her sword                                                                                                          |
| 64 Thiyya tharas       | 64 tharas of Thiyyar tharawads from Malabar (mainly Kannur and Kozhikode) stand at the temple (Wikipedia)                                                                                |
| Priests                | Vanamali Ashram: daily pujas by **Konkan priests from Mangalore**, not Namboodiri Brahmins. Namboodiris enter for the post-Bharani _punyaham_ (purification)                             |
| Daily forms            | At different hours she is worshipped as Maha Saraswati, Maha Lakshmi, and as Durga-Kannagi (Vanamali)                                                                                    |
| Photography            | Not permitted inside                                                                                                                                                                     |

## 7. Festivals

### 7.1 Bharani (Meenam / March–April)

The temple’s signature festival. Span: **Bharani of Kumbham** to **seven days after Bharani of Meenam**. Kerala Tourism PDF: ~7 ritual days; ~10,000 people. Largest congregation of oracles (_velichappad_) is often claimed here.

| Ritual                                          | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Kozhikkallu moodal**                          | Opening act. Traditionally a rooster sacrificed on red silk over stones around the idol — a symbol of Kali’s fight with Darika. Since the 1977–78 ban this is reduced to covering the stones with red silk. First-cock right is associated with the **Thacholi** clan (Vanamali)                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Kavu Theendal / Aswathy Kavutheendal**        | Flagged off when the **King of Kodungallur** unfurls a red umbrella. Oracles in red, with sickles / bamboo, run around the temple in trance, strike rafters, throw turmeric, coconuts, and offerings onto the inner quadrangle — ritually “polluting” the _kavu_. _Bharanippattu_ are raw, sometimes abusive ballads addressed to the goddess; tradition says she accepts them. Temple then closes about a week for purification |
| **Revathi vilakku**                             | Lighting of the traditional lamp                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Chandanapoti Charthal / Thrichandanacharthu** | Image smeared with sandal paste                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

After Theendal: premises swept, wells purified; Namboodiri priests perform _punyaham_; _pasu dhanam_ (gift of cows). Prasadam taken home: sandal mixed with turmeric, and _chantu_ (burnt-rice black paste).

**Website tone:** describe Bharani as ancient Shakta / folk devotion — not as a curiosity. State clearly that live sacrifice is **not** part of the sanctioned modern festival.

### 7.2 Thalappoli (Makaram / January–February)

Four days from the evening of **Makara Sankranti**. Elephant procession and _kathina vedi_ (barrel crackers). Women carry _thalam_ (rice, coconut-shell lamp). Wikipedia / Manorama / New Indian Express:

- Day 1: **Malayarayan** community arrives with _irumudi kettu_, turmeric and pepper, special poojas
- **Kudumbi** community with instruments; _Savasini puja_
- Day 3: procession from **Pathinettarayalam Kovilakam**
- Remaining days: Devaswom Board organises Thalappoli
- Sangam games are held in memory of the Sangam people (Wikipedia)

### 7.3 Other observances

e-Kshetra lists **Navaratri** and **Chandattam** (the yearly protective bath of the wooden image).

## 10. Voice and accuracy rules for the new site

1. Call her **Sree Kurumba / Kodungallur Amma / Bhadrakali** — all three are used by devotees.
2. Label legends as _itihasa_ / _sthalapurana_. Do not flatten Kannagi and Kali into one sentence without saying “tradition holds”.
3. Do not advertise animal sacrifice. Mention the 1978 ban if the old ritual is described.
4. Do not sensationalise _Bharanippattu_. Frame it as a permitted, ancient form of address to a mother who accepts even harsh speech.
5. Prefer official timings and contact over travel blogs when they disagree.
6. Photography of the inner sanctum is not allowed — do not invent or generate a “photograph of the murti”.
7. Administration credit: Cochin Devaswom Board, with the historical role of Kodungallur Kovilakam.
